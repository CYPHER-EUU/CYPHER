## Additional direct / adjacent competitors identified (Part A)

### Takeaway
Beyond the given list, the market includes (1) legacy-brand selfie-diagnostic tools from Neutrogena and L'Oréal/Vichy that are now mostly web-based rather than standalone apps, (2) a cluster of independent AI skin-analysis/beauty-tech startups in Europe (Dermaself in Italy, Haut.AI in Estonia, Skin Analytics and Skin AI focused on medical skin-cancer detection in UK/Spain), and (3) two under-the-radar French players — Skinmed and BeautyDecoded — plus the French DTC brand Typology, which are the closest things found to French-native rivals, though none matches Glowy's exact photo-AI-diagnosis + personalized-skincare-recommendation model one-for-one. SkinSort, despite the name match to "Skin Sort," is actually an ingredient/product scanner (Yuka/INCI Beauty-type), not a photo-diagnosis app. No evidence of a Apple Health native skin-diagnosis feature was found.

## What's the general market growth trajectory, and is this still an attractive/crowded category?

### Takeaway
Multiple independent market-research estimates point to strong double-digit growth for AI-driven skincare/beauty-tech through the mid-2030s, and the adjacent European personalized-skincare market is already sizable (~$7.3B in 2024). Beauty-sector M&A hit a record deal count in 2024 with skincare/beauty-tech/AI-personalization named as a priority growth area for 2025, but deal *values* have fallen sharply, and the category is explicitly described in trade press as "saturated" at the brand-tool level — suggesting an attractive but increasingly crowded space.

### Cited Findings
- Global "AI in skincare" market estimated at **USD 4.92 billion in 2025**, projected to reach **USD 5.84 billion in 2026** and **~USD 27.41 billion by 2035** (CAGR 18.74% from 2026–2035); App Store is cited as the fastest-growing sub-segment (21.4% CAGR 2026–2035); North America leads currently, Asia-Pacific is the fastest-growing region. — [Towards Healthcare](https://www.towardshealthcare.com/insights/ai-in-skincare-market-sizing)
- A narrower "AI Skin Coach App" market is sized at **$1.8 billion in 2025**, projected to reach **$7.8 billion by 2034** (CAGR 17.2%). Source is a lesser-known market-research publisher (MarketIntelo); treat this figure as a secondary/lower-confidence estimate alongside the Towards Healthcare figure above. — [MarketIntelo](https://marketintelo.com/report/ai-skin-coach-app-market)
- The **European personalized/customized skincare market** was valued at **$7.3 billion in 2024**, projected to reach **$13.6 billion by 2034** (CAGR 6.4%); UK is the largest national market in the region, Germany the fastest-growing; named European players include Haut.AI, Revieve, L'Oréal's "Magnify," and the "Skinome Project." — [GM Insights](https://www.gminsights.com/industry-analysis/europe-personalized-customized-skin-care-market)
- Global beauty & personal care **M&A hit a record 355 deals in 2024, up 40% YoY**, but average deal value fell ~60% over two years (from ~$400M in 2022 to ~$160M in 2024); 80% of industry operators expect both deal volume and valuations to grow further in 2025; skincare/dermocosmetics is the top-ranked category for expected deal volume (+8%), with beauty-tech, AI, and treatment personalization specifically called out as high-interest areas. — [GCI Magazine / Cosmetics & Toiletries, "The 2024–2025 Beauty M&A Forecast"](https://www.gcimagazine.com/consumers-markets/article/22878885/the-20242025-beauty-ma-forecast)
- L'Oréal's own trade press has explicitly described the category as crowded: a 2019 Cosmetics Business headline reads "L'Oréal adds to **saturated market** of AI skin diagnostic tools." — [Cosmetics Business](https://cosmeticsbusiness.com/l-or-al-adds-to-saturated-market-of-ai-skin-diagnostic-tools--152044)

### Inferences
- The category shows a bifurcated structure: large, well-capitalized beauty conglomerates (L'Oréal, Shiseido, Neutrogena/Kenvue) offer free or near-free AI-diagnosis tools as customer-acquisition funnels, while independent/venture-backed players (Dermaself, Haut.AI, Revieve, MDacne, Curology, Proven, etc.) must monetize the diagnosis-to-recommendation loop directly (subscriptions, DTC product sales, B2B licensing) — meaning growth projections at the "market" level may overstate the addressable opportunity for an independent app competing against free brand tools.
- Falling average M&A deal values alongside record deal *counts* suggests consolidation is happening mostly among smaller/earlier-stage players (bolt-on acquisitions) rather than mega-deals — consistent with the Formel Skin (Germany, acquired by Manual, Oct 2025) and Amyris/Beauty Labs examples found.

## Consumer behavior trends: usage, trust in AI diagnosis, and privacy concerns

### Takeaway
Available survey data (mostly medical/dermatology-context rather than cosmetic-app-specific) shows consumers are broadly receptive to AI involvement in skin assessment and willing to share health data under privacy safeguards, but regulators (CNIL in France) and legal commentators flag selfie/facial-skin images as sensitive/health-category data requiring explicit consent, and real-world controversies (Perfect Corp/YouCam) show trust can be fragile when data-handling claims are challenged.

### Cited Findings
- In a survey of ~300 patients with suspected melanoma (about half with a prior skin-cancer diagnosis), **94% would welcome AI as an augmenter of their dermatologist's diagnosis**, and **more than 40% would trust a standalone AI system's diagnosis** on its own. — [PMC / Frontiers in Medicine study, via HealthExec](https://healthexec.com/topics/precision-medicine/patients-possible-skin-cancer-trust-ai-help)
- In the same patient-population research stream, **88% of patients said they would make their own health data available (if identities were protected)** to support development of AI-based medical applications. — [Frontiers in Medicine](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2020.00233/pdf)
- Legal/privacy commentary: uploaded skincare-app selfies "may constitute special categories of personal data" (e.g., images showing rosacea, rashes, acne), subject to strict processing rules; companies are urged to secure explicit consent, encrypt data, and comply with data-protection regulation given the sensitivity of facial/skin images. — [GovInfoSecurity](https://www.govinfosecurity.com/ai-meets-personalized-skincare-data-privacy-in-tow-a-26776), [NJORD Law (Estonia)](https://www.njordlaw.com/the-use-of-ai-in-dermatology-and-skincare)
- France's CNIL has issued specific guidance/reminders on apps using facial scanning in light of GDPR, emphasizing that users should be able to verify what data is collected, how it's used, and for how long; purely on-device/local processing with no external transmission can fall outside GDPR's scope, but most connected AI-diagnosis apps (which upload images for server-side analysis) would not qualify for that exemption and face fuller GDPR obligations given health-adjacent data. — [CNIL](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/node/1356)
- Real-world trust controversy: Perfect Corp (maker of YouCam Makeup/YouCam Perfect, already on the known competitor list) was publicly accused of transferring user data to Chinese servers for resale; the company denied this and said data is hosted on AWS outside China; nonetheless, an Indian "Remove China App" campaign placed YouCam apps on a banned list in June 2020 — illustrating how quickly trust issues can escalate for facial-data apps even when a company disputes the allegations. — [Cosmetics Business](https://cosmeticsbusiness.com/perfect-corp-denies-misleading-privacy-allegations-made-against-its-youcam-beauty-apps--166537), [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20200617005254/en/Perfect-Corp.-Reiterates-Its-Commitment-to-Data-Protection-and-Secure-Handling-of-User-Data)
- TikTok's skincare content category grew **14x globally over nine months in 2022**, and **#skincareroutine has over 54 billion views**, indicating very high organic consumer engagement with skincare-diagnosis/advice content on social platforms (a major discovery/acquisition channel adjacent to dedicated apps). — [W Magazine / search aggregation](https://www.wmagazine.com/gallery/best-tiktok-skincare-products-cerave-the-ordinary-tatcha/amp/)

### Inferences
- The strong stated willingness to share health/skin data (88–94% in melanoma-patient surveys) likely overstates general-population comfort with cosmetic apps, since these surveys were conducted among patients with an existing serious medical motivation (cancer risk) — a different risk/benefit calculus than a general consumer deciding whether to upload a selfie to a skincare-recommendation app. This gap between clinical-context trust data and cosmetic-app-specific trust data is a meaningful limitation researchers and the report-writer should flag.
- For a France/Europe-targeted app like Glowy, CNIL's explicit guidance on facial-scanning apps and GDPR's treatment of health-adjacent data suggest that clear, prominent consent flows and transparent data-retention/deletion policies are not just best practice but a likely competitive differentiator and compliance necessity.

### Gaps
- No cosmetic-app-specific (as opposed to medical-dermatology-context) survey data on consumer trust in AI skin diagnosis or willingness to upload selfies for skincare (non-medical) purposes was found. This is a meaningful gap — the figures above are the best available proxies but are from a different context (melanoma/skin-cancer risk assessment, not cosmetic personalization).
- No specific, citable statistic for "how many people use skin-scan/skincare-quiz apps" (e.g., as a % of smartphone users or skincare consumers) was found; the Statista dataset that likely contains this was inaccessible (blocked by network egress in this environment). ## Common user complaints/pain points with skin-diagnosis apps (differentiation opportunities for Glowy)

### Takeaway
Across both clinical skin-cancer-detection apps and commercial skincare apps, the most consistently documented pain points are (1) diagnostic inaccuracy/false reassurance, (2) subscription and billing friction (unexpected recurring charges, hard-to-cancel plans), (3) data-privacy distrust around facial-image handling, and (4) a structural risk of generic or mismatched recommendations when the underlying AI misclassifies skin type/condition — all of which represent concrete differentiation angles for a new entrant.

### Cited Findings
- A University of Pittsburgh study found that **3 of 4 tested smartphone skin-lesion-assessment apps gave incorrect "unconcerning" results for 30% or more of melanomas** they were shown; the apps are typically free/low-cost and face no regulatory validation requirement, and are marketed to non-clinical users — creating a real risk that users forgo a doctor visit based on a false-negative app result. (Note: this research is specifically about skin-*cancer* screening apps, a more safety-critical sub-category than cosmetic skin-analysis apps, but illustrates the general "AI photo diagnosis can be wrong and users may over-trust it" risk pattern relevant to any skin-condition-detecting app.) — [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/health/smartphone-apps-for-skin-cancer-how-accurate-are-they.amp), [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/podcast/episode/skin-cancer-spotting-apps-miss-thei-13-01-16/)
- MDacne (already on the known list) has an overall strong reputation (**88.6% positive sentiment across 18,000+ reviews**; **Trustpilot score 4.5/5 from 6,302 reviews**) but recurring negative-review themes include: **skin irritation/dryness from recommended products**, **unexpected recurring subscription charges and difficult cancellation**, **international delivery delays and unexpected import fees**, and **"purging phase" complaints citing inadequate live human medical support** during product adjustment periods. — [JustUseApp aggregation](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1044050208/mdacne-custom-acne-treatment/similar), [Outlook India](https://outlookindia.com/hub4health/mdacne-supplement-bad-reviews-and-complaints)
- Perfect Corp (YouCam apps, already on the known list) faced a public controversy over claims it transferred user facial/selfie data to Chinese servers for resale; the company denied the claims and said data is hosted on AWS servers outside China, but the controversy led to the apps being placed on an India-based "Remove China App" ban list in June 2020 — a concrete example of a facial-data privacy trust failure in this exact product category. — [Cosmetics Business](https://cosmeticsbusiness.com/perfect-corp-denies-misleading-privacy-allegations-made-against-its-youcam-beauty-apps--166537)
- Industry/legal commentary consistently flags **generic or mismatched recommendations** as a risk: "an AI skincare analysis tool that misidentifies skin type—such as claiming oily skin when it's actually dry—would result in product recommendations that exacerbate skin issues rather than improve them," with associated **liability concerns when inaccurate advice causes harm**. — [Mirrar blog, aggregating industry commentary](https://mirrar.com/blogs/ai-skin-analysis-how-technology-is-transforming-skincare-solutions)
- A French-language 2026 press roundup of skincare apps (summary only; direct article access was blocked) characterizes the market as having "exploded in the past three years" with many entrants, but notes that "scientific rigor often doesn't match the promises made" across the category. — [lessentieldeleco.fr, via search-result summary](https://lessentieldeleco.fr/6552-applications-mobiles-skincare-quel-app-choisir-pour-prendre-soin-de-sa-peau-en-2026/) (secondary-sourced; direct fetch blocked, flagged as lower-confidence)

### Inferences
- The recurring complaint pattern (accuracy doubts, subscription/billing friction, privacy distrust, generic recommendations) maps directly onto differentiation levers a new entrant like Glowy could emphasize: transparent/validated diagnostic methodology, straightforward non-dark-pattern subscription terms, clear on-device-first or strongly GDPR-compliant data handling (especially relevant for a France/Europe launch given CNIL's explicit facial-scanning guidance), and recommendation logic that is explainable (why a product was suggested) rather than a black-box score — echoing what reviewers already praise in Yuka/INCI-Beauty-style ingredient transparency tools and what L'Oréal's own Skin Genius messaging emphasizes ("explains why each product has been recommended").
- Because the most severe accuracy failures documented are in the medical skin-*cancer*-screening sub-category (not cosmetic skin-condition apps), Glowy should be careful to scope its own accuracy claims and marketing language conservatively, and research/legal review should confirm Glowy does not inadvertently market itself as a medical diagnostic tool, which carries a different (higher) regulatory and liability bar in both the complaint literature and CNIL/GDPR health-data guidance found above.
