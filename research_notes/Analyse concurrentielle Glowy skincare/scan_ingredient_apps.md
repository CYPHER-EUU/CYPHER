# Ingredient/Product Scanning Apps (Yuka, INCI Beauty, Codecheck, EWG Skin Deep, Think Dirty) as Competitors to Glowy

Scope note: these apps are **barcode/ingredient-scoring tools**, structurally different from Glowy (skin diagnosis + personalized routine). The research below is organized to (a) establish each app's profile across the six requested dimensions and (b) directly answer the four key questions. Where data is stale (pre-2023) or sources conflict, this is flagged explicitly rather than reconciled by guesswork.

## Value proposition / positioning of each app

### Takeaway
All five apps position themselves as **consumer-empowerment / transparency tools that score a product's composition** ("scan and know what's really inside"), not as personal skin/health advisors. None frames itself around diagnosing the user; all frame themselves around decoding the product.

### Cited Findings
- Yuka's own positioning: it "reveals the detailed composition of food and cosmetic products, providing precise information about nutrients, additives, and potentially harmful substances," scanned via barcode — a single app covering both food and cosmetics. — [Futura-Sciences / search synthesis](https://www.futura-sciences.com/tech/definitions/logiciels-yuka-27752/)
- Yuka describes itself as "100% independent," taking no funding from brands or manufacturers, funded instead by its Premium subscription and a voluntary "brand improvement" program plus "YukaCoin" virtual currency — a trust/independence-first positioning. — [Yuka business model page, via search synthesis](https://yuka.io/en/quiz/business-model/)
- INCI Beauty: "the most complete application on the market that helps you decipher the content of cosmetics and take back the power over your consumption" — scan/search barcode or ingredient name, built by a French company in Cournon-d'Auvergne, in scientific partnership with the Sigma engineering school (Clermont-Ferrand) since 2017. — [archives.internetscout.org summary](https://archives.internetscout.org/r95605/inci_beauty_app); [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/inci-beauty/about)
- INCI Beauty states no cosmetics brand can directly or indirectly influence its ratings; it is funded by its parent price-comparison company Touslesprix.com (created ~25 years ago, i.e. ~2001), plus a Premium tier, advertising, and a "Pro" offer for brands/professionals. — [INCI Beauty funding page, via search synthesis](https://incibeauty.com/funding)
- Codecheck: "a personal and easy-to-use shopping assistant for healthy and sustainable consumption" that scans a barcode and shows ratings/alternatives for food or cosmetics; its ratings are built on assessments by its scientific department and independent experts (German Allergy and Asthma Association, Consumer Center Hamburg, Greenpeace Switzerland, WWF). — [CodeCheck "Our Story" / company page, via search synthesis](https://codecheck-app.com/our-story/); [S-GE case study](https://www.s-ge.com/en/article/news/codecheck-receives-fresh-capital)
- EWG's Healthy Living app combines EWG's Skin Deep®, Food Scores, and Guide to Healthy Cleaning databases to deliver a 1–10 safety score (1 = best) on ~200,000 products via barcode scan or name search — a "shop safer" tool, U.S.-centric and standards-based (EWG's own "Unacceptable" ingredient list). — [EWG release, via search synthesis](https://cdn3.ewg.org/release/new-ewg-healthy-living-app-includes-healthy-cleaners); [EWG App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/app/id703155791)
- Think Dirty "evaluates products based solely on their chemical content, not brand reputation or marketing claims," delivering barcode-scan ingredient ratings; it markets itself with awards (App Store "Changemaker" 2023, Fast Company World Changing Ideas Award 2020). — [Think Dirty summary, via search synthesis](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/think-dirty-shop-clean/id687176839)

### Inferences
- The entire category's positioning is "informed consumer / anti-greenwashing," built on independence-from-brands as the core trust signal — a different value axis than Glowy's personalization-and-results axis.

### Gaps
- No direct, dated quote was found from Yuka's or INCI Beauty's own 2024-2026 marketing copy (e.g., current App Store description text) confirming whether their positioning language has shifted toward personalization; app-store pages themselves were unreachable (see Gaps under scanning method, below).

## Diagnostic/scanning method — do Yuka or INCI Beauty do personal skin diagnosis, or only product scoring? (Key Question 1)

### Takeaway
Both Yuka and INCI Beauty are **product/ingredient scoring tools only** — barcode scan (or name/photo search) → ingredient-list lookup → a generic 0–100 (Yuka) or 0–20 (INCI Beauty) score applied the same way to every user. Neither performs a personal skin diagnosis (no skin-type questionnaire feeding a tailored score, no photo-based skin analysis). This is corroborated by a 2026 French-press comparison piece that explicitly criticizes the category for this limitation.

### Cited Findings
- Yuka's method: scan a product's barcode; the app returns a 0–100 "healthiness"/safety score plus an additive-by-additive breakdown and suggested "healthier alternatives" — the same scoring logic for every user regardless of skin type or health profile. — [search synthesis of Yuka business-model/help pages](https://help.yuka.io/l/en/article/b5wkrfepnp-why-are-there-different-tariffs)
- INCI Beauty's method: scan a barcode or search a product/ingredient by name; results are shown on a 0–20 scale (e.g., a vanilla deodorant scored 11.7/20, "light green/satisfactory" badge); each ingredient is clickable for a detail sheet (function, origin, restrictions). — [search synthesis; ToysGuide.fr example](https://www.tomsguide.fr/notation-des-cosmetiques-pourquoi-les-applications-donnent-des-resultats-differents/)
- INCI Beauty free-account features: favorites, product photos, unlimited history, **shared cosmetic "routines"** (i.e., a routine as a saved/shareable product list, not an algorithmically generated one), "bathroom" management, alternative-product suggestions, consultation history, and ingredient alerts — none of these involve a skin-type questionnaire or photo-based skin diagnosis. — [search synthesis of incibeauty.com/features](https://incibeauty.com/features)
- Explicit third-party critique (2026 French comparison article on skincare apps): "the system of scoring is identical for all users, regardless of their skin type, sensitivities, or objectives" — a direct statement that INCI Beauty (grouped with Yuka) does not personalize to the individual. The same source states that "the majority of popular applications today... do not provide true personalization," and defines true personalization as crossing product composition with an individual skin profile (type, sensitivity, active concerns, intolerances) — which these two apps do not do. — [lessentieldeleco.fr, via search synthesis](https://lessentieldeleco.fr/6552-applications-mobiles-skincare-quel-app-choisir-pour-prendre-soin-de-sa-peau-en-2026/)
- Codecheck's method: scan a barcode; app shows whether the product contains substances of concern, rates >6,000 ingredients against a >2-million-product database, and suggests alternatives; personalization is limited to toggleable dietary/property filters (gluten, lactose, microplastics, palm oil, silicones, vegan/vegetarian) — again, attribute filtering, not a skin or health diagnosis. — [S-GE case study, via search synthesis](https://www.s-ge.com/en/article/news/codecheck-receives-fresh-capital)
- EWG Healthy Living: barcode scan or name search → 1–10 safety score against EWG's "Unacceptable" ingredients list — no personal skin diagnosis. — [search synthesis](https://cdn3.ewg.org/release/new-ewg-healthy-living-app-includes-healthy-cleaners)
- Think Dirty: barcode scan → ingredient-based rating; evaluates the product "based solely on chemical content," explicitly not on the user. — [search synthesis](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/think-dirty-shop-clean/id687176839)

### Inferences
- None of the five apps competes with Glowy on the "diagnose my skin, then recommend a routine for me" axis today; they compete on "tell me if this product is safe/clean," which is a complementary rather than fully overlapping job-to-be-done. A user could plausibly use Yuka/INCI Beauty to vet products that Glowy recommends — i.e., coexistence rather than pure substitution — but they also address the same underlying anxiety (ingredient safety) that drives part of Glowy's value, which is a competitive-adjacency risk worth flagging to the writer.

### Gaps
- Could not directly access incibeauty.com, apps.apple.com, or Yuka's own site/press kit due to the research environment's egress proxy blocking these domains (EGRESS_BLOCKED errors on every attempt); all INCI Beauty/Yuka feature and ratings claims above are therefore sourced from third-party summaries and search-engine synthesis of those pages rather than direct primary-source verification. This should be re-verified by someone with normal web access before being treated as fully authoritative.

Yuka's user base is in the **tens of millions and growing fast internationally** — most recent available figures (2025) put it at roughly 40–65 million users across ~12 countries (sources disagree on the exact figure, both from 2025), with strong app-store ratings (~4.7/5 on both stores). INCI Beauty's current scale is much harder to pin down: the only concrete figures found are old (low hundreds of thousands of downloads, 2017-era) and the only current-ish figure found — "~4.5 million users" — appears in a source actually describing a different, outdated Yuka data point, so it should not be trusted as INCI Beauty's number. Codecheck's most recent concrete figures are also dated (2020: 4.5M users / >10M downloads; a later undated figure cites >7M downloads and 2M monthly active users).

## Recent (2024-2026) skin-diagnosis or personalization feature additions (Key Question 3)

### Takeaway
No evidence was found that Yuka or INCI Beauty has launched a skin-diagnosis, skin-type questionnaire, or AI-personalized-routine feature in 2024-2026. The most relevant 2026 French press coverage of the skincare-app category explicitly frames this as a *gap* in the incumbents (Yuka, INCI Beauty) that newer, personalization-first apps are positioned to fill — i.e., as of this research, Yuka/INCI Beauty remain product-scoring tools, not diagnosis tools, and the competitive opening for Glowy-type apps appears to still be open.

### Cited Findings
- A dedicated 2026 comparison article on "which skincare app to choose" states that today's popular apps (grouping Yuka and INCI Beauty together) are "misunderstood" in that they are taken for personalization tools when they are not, and that "true personalization" — crossing a product against an individual skin profile (type, sensitivity, concerns, intolerances) — is what differentiates the newer generation of apps from simple ingredient scanners. This implies INCI Beauty/Yuka had not closed that gap as of the article's writing. — [lessentieldeleco.fr, "Applications mobiles skincare: quel app choisir pour prendre soin de sa peau en 2026," via search synthesis](https://lessentieldeleco.fr/6552-applications-mobiles-skincare-quel-app-choisir-pour-prendre-soin-de-sa-peau-en-2026/)
- Separately, a broader "Beauty Tech 2025" industry piece notes an industry-wide trend toward AI-powered personalization, ultra-precise skin diagnostics, and customized skincare recommendation algorithms — but this describes the category/competitive landscape in general (e.g., L'Oréal Modiface/SkinConsultAI, Perfect Corp's AI+3D tools, Sephora's own in-store/online "diagnostic peau") rather than confirming that Yuka or INCI Beauty specifically shipped such a feature. — [ecomundo.eu "Beauty Tech en 2025," via search synthesis](https://ecomundo.eu/blog/beauty-tech-2025)
- No press release, blog post, or news article was found announcing a Yuka or INCI Beauty skin-diagnosis, photo-based skin analysis, or personalized-routine-builder feature for 2024, 2025, or 2026.

### Inferences
- As of October 2026 (this research date), Yuka and INCI Beauty have not moved into Glowy's core turf (skin diagnosis + personalized routine). The competitive threat they pose to Glowy is indirect (overlapping ingredient-safety anxiety, shared "informed consumer" audience) rather than direct feature-for-feature competition — but given Yuka's scale, resources, and now-profitable model, a future move into personalization cannot be ruled out and is worth monitoring.

### Gaps
- This is an absence-of-evidence finding, not confirmed absence-of-feature: it is possible a minor personalization feature (e.g., a basic skin-type filter) was added that simply did not surface in search results. A direct check of the current Yuka and INCI Beauty app UI (not just press coverage) would be the most reliable way to close this gap.
- Could not identify named competing apps that lessentieldeleco.fr implies *do* offer true personalization (the source describing the gap was only accessible via search snippet, not full text, so the "better" apps it likely names alongside Yuka/INCI Beauty were not captured).

## Codecheck's positioning relative to Yuka (Key Question 4)

### Takeaway
Codecheck is essentially **the same product concept as Yuka — barcode scan → ingredient safety score → healthier alternatives, covering both food and cosmetics — but older, Swiss-founded, and focused on the DACH market (Germany, Austria, Switzerland) plus UK/US**, whereas Yuka is French-founded and now dominant across France/Belgium/Spain/Switzerland with a growing US presence. Codecheck is smaller in scale and its public growth data is noticeably staler (last concrete figures ~2020-21) than Yuka's (regularly updated through 2025).

### Cited Findings
- Codecheck was founded in Zurich in 2010 (as an association/database), with CodeCheck AG formed in 2014 — i.e., several years before Yuka (founded/launched January 2017 in France). — [search synthesis, Wikipedia/Deutsche-Startups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CodeCheck)
- Functionally near-identical to Yuka: barcode scan, ingredient-level ratings (>6,000 ingredients rated, >2 million products in database), suggested alternatives, free core app with optional personalization filters (gluten/lactose/microplastics/palm oil/silicones/vegan). — [S-GE case study, via search synthesis](https://www.s-ge.com/en/article/news/codecheck-receives-fresh-capital)
- Codecheck's geographic base is explicitly DACH-first: user base historically concentrated in Germany, Austria, Switzerland, with later expansion to the UK and USA (a 2017-era press release is titled "CodeCheck app – The popular personal shopping assistant... now in UK"). — [search synthesis](https://codecheck-app.com/press/codecheck-app-the-popular-personal-shopping-assistant-for-a-conscious-lifestyle-now-in-uk)
- One search result explicitly frames Yuka's German-market entry as following/competing in the space Codecheck already occupied: "the French product-scanning app Yuka has been available in Germany for about a year... Yuka recognizes 75% of products available in Switzerland" — i.e., Yuka expanded into Codecheck's home turf (DACH/Switzerland) rather than the reverse. — [search synthesis, Le Temps-style coverage](https://www.letemps.ch/economie/yuka-lapplication-analyse-produits-alimentaires)
- Codecheck's ratings methodology leans on named third-party validators (German Allergy and Asthma Association, Consumer Center Hamburg, Greenpeace Switzerland, WWF) — a somewhat more institutional-partnership-based credibility model than Yuka's "fully independent, no outside validators named" framing. — [search synthesis](https://www.s-ge.com/en/article/news/codecheck-receives-fresh-capital)
- Scale gap: Codecheck's best available figures (2020-21) show ~4.5-7 million total users/downloads and ~2 million monthly actives, versus Yuka's 2025 figures of 40-65 million users — Yuka is roughly an order of magnitude larger on the most recent comparable data available for each. — [search synthesis, compiled from above sources]

### Inferences
- Codecheck reads as "Yuka's Swiss/German predecessor/counterpart" rather than a head-to-head rival in the same national market: in France specifically (Glowy's likely home market), Codecheck has negligible visible presence in the sources found, while Yuka is the dominant incumbent. For a France/EU-focused competitive analysis, Codecheck is more relevant as a DACH-market analog/precedent than as a direct competitive threat to a France-based app like Glowy.

### Gaps
- No direct statement was found from Codecheck itself characterizing its relationship or rivalry with Yuka (the "Yuka expanded into Codecheck's turf" framing is an inference from juxtaposed facts, not a quoted competitive statement from either company).
- Codecheck's presence (if any) in France specifically was not established — it may be effectively absent from the French market, but this was not directly confirmed.

## Monetization models (summary across apps)

### Takeaway
All apps in this set use a **freemium model**: free core scanning/scoring, with revenue from (a) a low-cost premium subscription (ads removal and/or minor convenience features), (b) a B2B/"Pro" offering to brands, and in some cases (c) e-commerce/price-comparison affiliate revenue or voluntary donations — notably, none of these apps relies on an expensive premium subscription tier comparable to typical skincare-app pricing.

### Cited Findings
- Yuka: free for barcode scan, 0-100 score, additive breakdown, and alternatives (no ads in free tier); Yuka Plus premium adds a text-search bar (search without scanning), offline scanning mode, and custom dietary/ingredient alerts; priced as "pay what you want" — $10/$15/$20 per year in the US App Store, €10-20/year in the Euro area, with identical features at all three price points (an explicit "support us more if you can" model). Revenue also reportedly includes a "brand improvement" program and a YukaCoin virtual currency (per Yuka's own business-model explainer). — [search synthesis, Yuka help center + business-model page](https://help.yuka.io/l/en/article/b5wkrfepnp-why-are-there-different-tariffs)
- INCI Beauty: free app; Premium tier at €1.50/month removes ads; the company self-funds primarily via its sister price-comparison site Touslesprix.com, plus a "Pro" subscription for cosmetics brands/professionals and received a one-time €100,000 interest-free loan (BPI France + Auvergne Region, 2019). — [search synthesis](https://www.clubic.com/telecharger-fiche442212-inci-beauty.html); [incibeauty.com/funding, via search synthesis](https://incibeauty.com/funding)
- Codecheck: free app with optional personalization filters; monetization specifics beyond its venture funding rounds (2014: ~€1M/CHF1.1M; 2017: CHF1.3M) were not detailed in sources found — no explicit ad/premium/affiliate breakdown was located. — [search synthesis](https://www.deutsche-startups.de/2017/11/20/codecheck-bekommt-13-millionen-spendiert/)
- EWG Healthy Living: free app; EWG is a nonprofit, so monetization is via the broader organization's donation model rather than app-specific subscriptions (not independently confirmed in detail from sources found).
- Think Dirty: free app ("Shop Clean"); specific monetization details (ads, premium tier, affiliate/retail links) were not detailed in sources found beyond its consumer-facing positioning.

### Inferences
- The category norm is "free + very cheap optional premium (ads removal, minor convenience) + B2B/brand revenue," which sets a very low price-anchor for any paid tier in this space — relevant if Glowy's own monetization (likely a more substantial subscription, given personalized routines/products) needs to be justified against consumer expectations shaped by Yuka's €10-20/year or INCI Beauty's €1.50/month.

### Gaps
- No confirmed detail on Codecheck's, EWG's, or Think Dirty's specific monetization mechanics (ads networks, affiliate commerce, B2B data licensing) beyond what's noted above.

## Key differentiating strengths vs. a skin-diagnosis + personalized-routine app like Glowy

### Takeaway
The scanning apps' core strengths are **trust/independence branding, mass-market scale and brand recognition (Yuka especially), breadth (food + cosmetics in one app for Yuka/Codecheck), community/crowdsourced engagement (INCI Beauty), and institutional/NGO credibility (Codecheck, EWG)** — strengths built on transparency and independence rather than on personalization or skincare expertise, which is where Glowy's positioning diverges.

### Cited Findings
- Yuka's brand strength rests on explicit no-brand-funding independence messaging and high (4.7/5) app-store ratings across hundreds of thousands of reviews, built over ~8 years into a default "go-to" scanning habit for tens of millions of EU consumers. — [search synthesis, as cited above]
- Yuka's dual food+cosmetics scope in a single app gives it a broader daily-use occasion (grocery shopping and skincare/makeup shopping both) than a skincare-only app, reinforcing habitual use. — [search synthesis]
- INCI Beauty differentiates on **community features**: discussion with other users, shared "routines" (saved product lists), and a direct scientific partnership with an engineering school for its ingredient methodology — a more community/forum-like engagement layer than Yuka. — [search synthesis, incibeauty.com/features summary](https://incibeauty.com/features)
- Codecheck differentiates on **named institutional validators** (Greenpeace, WWF, consumer-protection and allergy associations) backing its ratings, which may read as more "independently verified" to some users than Yuka's self-described independence. — [search synthesis](https://www.s-ge.com/en/article/news/codecheck-receives-fresh-capital)
- EWG Skin Deep's differentiator is its decades-old, large, specifically cosmetics-focused ingredient hazard database (EWG is primarily known in the US for Skin Deep specifically, pre-dating its unified Healthy Living app) and a strict "Unacceptable ingredients" standard — strong credibility as an ingredient-safety reference in the US market. — [search synthesis]
- Think Dirty's differentiator is a simple, award-recognized consumer-facing brand ("Shop Clean") with explicit messaging that it rates "chemical content, not brand reputation or marketing claims," appealing to a clean-beauty-skeptical-of-marketing audience. — [search synthesis](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/think-dirty-shop-clean/id687176839)

### Inferences
- None of these apps currently builds a skincare routine, assesses an individual's skin, or recommends products matched to a skin profile — their strength is pure product-level transparency at massive scale (Yuka) or with strong institutional backing (Codecheck, EWG). Glowy's differentiation versus all of them is the personalization and diagnostic layer; the risk is that Yuka in particular already owns the "ingredient trust" mindshare that Glowy would also need to build if it wants its own product scoring to be trusted independently of its routine recommendations.

### Gaps
- No data found on cross-usage overlap (e.g., what % of Glowy's likely target users already use Yuka) — this would require primary user research, not available via this search-based method.

## Primary geographic market and international expansion

### Takeaway
Yuka is France-founded and still France/EU-centered but now meaningfully international (12 countries, ~20M US users, Belgium/Switzerland/Spain). INCI Beauty is France-only/France-centered with no evidence of international expansion found. Codecheck is Switzerland-founded and DACH-centered (Germany/Austria/Switzerland) with secondary UK/US presence. EWG Skin Deep/Healthy Living and Think Dirty are both US/North America-centered with no confirmed significant France/EU footprint.

### Cited Findings
- Yuka: available in ~12 countries as of 2025, explicitly including France, Belgium, Switzerland, Spain, and the United States; opened a US office with CEO Julie Chapon based there, driving ~20 million US users. — [search synthesis, as cited above]
- INCI Beauty: French company (Cournon-d'Auvergne, Auvergne region), scientific partnership with a French engineering school; no evidence found of operations or marketing outside France. — [search synthesis, as cited above]
