# US-Based Personalized Skincare Subscription/D2C Apps — Competitive Profiles (Curology, Proven Skincare, Trove Skin, Function of Beauty/Atolla, + others)

Scope note: five named targets were Trove Skin, Curology, Proven Skincare, Functionof, and Atolla. Research found two important structural facts that reshape this set: (1) no US company named simply "Trove Skin"/"TroveSkin" could be found — the only TroveSkin app is Singapore-headquartered and Southeast-Asia-focused; and (2) "Functionof" could not be confirmed as a distinct brand separate from **Function of Beauty**, which **acquired Atolla** in August 2021 — meaning two of the five assigned names are now one corporate family, not two independent competitors. Both points are flagged in detail below.

## Company Profile: Curology

### Takeaway
Curology is the most medically serious player in the set: a dermatologist-founded, San Francisco-based telehealth company (founded 2014) that has licensed medical providers prescribe individually compounded prescription creams (including tretinoin) via async photo/questionnaire consultation, sold on a ~$30-35/month subscription, has served 5.5M+ patients, and is explicitly US-only with no international shipping — making it a strong playbook reference but zero direct geographic threat to a France/EU-focused app today.

### Cited Findings
- Curology is a San Francisco-based company founded in 2014 that offers personalized acne care through online consultations, connecting patients with a licensed medical provider who prescribes a custom-compounded cream (up to three active ingredients) shipped monthly along with a cleanser and moisturizer — [altss.com Curology profile](https://altss.com/profile/curology.md)
- Pricing: HydroTretRx (personalized tretinoin formula) is $34.95/month ($69.90 per 60-day shipment, free shipping); Custom FormulaRx (non-tretinoin personalized prescription formula) is $29.95/month ($59.90 per shipment) — [Curology Pricing support article](https://support.curology.com/article/58-price)
- Funding: Curology closed a $15M Series A led by SignalFire (2017); an earlier/related $15M round was led by Advance Vixeid Partners with Sherpa Capital and Forerunner Ventures participating (sources did not clearly disambiguate round sequencing/labels) — [Clay.com Curology funding dossier](https://clay.com/dossier/curology-funding); [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/teledermatology-company-curology-raises-15m-scale-direct-consumer-subscription-skincare)
- Scale: Curology has treated 5.5+ million patients as of recent data (patient count had grown past 4 million by December 2022) — [Curology press/company materials via search synthesis]; Curology has 423 total employees per Built In's company profile — [Built In Curology](https://builtin.com/company/curology)
- Regulatory: In 2022 the FDA issued a warning letter to Curology for labeling its prescription-strength cream as an over-the-counter cosmetic product rather than a prescription drug; Curology revised its labeling and continued operations — [search synthesis citing FDA warning-letter coverage]
- Reviews: Curology has a 4.1/5 average on Trustpilot; 4.3/5 on Reviews.io (1,359 reviews, 84% recommend); 4.38/5 on Influenster (252 reviews); no verified Apple/Google app-store star rating was found in this research pass — [Reviews.io Curology](https://www.reviews.io/company-reviews/store/curology); [Influenster](https://www.influenster.com/reviews/curology-94)
- Geography: Curology cannot ship outside the US, not even to Canada; it is only licensed to see patients in select US states and has no stated timeline for international expansion — [search synthesis citing Curology support/International shipments policy](https://support.curology.com/article/77-international-shipments)

### Inferences
- Curology's FDA warning letter is a direct, citable data point that its product is legally a prescription drug (not a cosmetic) — this is the clearest regulatory evidence among all five companies that a compounded, dermatologist-prescribed personalized skincare product triggers real drug-law exposure, a relevant risk/opportunity signal for any EU competitor considering a prescription-based model.
- Being licensed only in "select US states" (rather than nationwide) suggests Curology's telehealth model is itself constrained by US state-by-state medical licensing — a structural hint that replicating this model across EU member states (each with its own medical/pharmacy regulator) would be at least as complex, if not more.

### Gaps
- Exact split between the two $15M rounds (which is "Series A" vs "Series B", and the precise 2016/2017 dates and full cap table) was not cleanly resolved from available sources — treat the "~$30M+ raised across two $15M rounds" framing as directionally correct but not fully reconciled.
- No confirmed Apple App Store / Google Play star rating or review count was found (only third-party review-aggregator scores).
- No confirmed current total funding figure beyond the two $15M rounds (no evidence of rounds after 2017 was found; Curology may be profitable/bootstrapped since, but this is not confirmed either way).

## Company Profile: Proven Skincare

### Takeaway
Proven Skincare is an AI/data-driven (not photo-based) personalized skincare brand built around a proprietary "Skin Genome Project" database and a short quiz; it sells a non-prescription 3-piece subscription system at roughly $60-65/month, has raised a comparatively modest ~$21M in venture funding (plus a separate, uncertain-outcome $60M Reg A+ crowdfunding-style offering), and — notably — already ships directly into Canada, the UK and the EU, making it a live cross-border competitive presence in Europe today.

### Cited Findings
- Diagnostic approach: Proven uses a three-minute quiz building an individual profile from 45+ categories (skincare goals, geography, genetics, lifestyle); its algorithm factors in changing seasons, skin tolerance to actives, geographic moves, aging, and pregnancy — [Proven Skincare company materials via search synthesis]
- Proprietary data asset: Proven's "Skin Genome Project" / "Skin Genomic Database" is described as drawing on 20M+ customer testimonials, 8M online reviews, 4,000+ scientific papers, and 20,000 skincare ingredients, analyzed via machine learning and NLP; the project won an MIT AI award in 2018 — [search synthesis of Proven/Skin Genome Project coverage]
- Proven holds patents covering formulation decisions driven by geographic/environmental/seasonal data (water hardness, temperature, humidity, UV levels) — [Retail Dive: Proven second patent](https://www.retaildive.com/news/proven-second-patent-personalized-skin-care/652418/)
- Pricing: a two-month supply of cleanser, SPF, and night cream runs $129.99 (~$60-65/month); frequent promotions discount a first kit to ~$99; the core system is 3 products (cleanser, day moisturizer, night cream), sold as an ongoing subscription — [MySubscriptionAddiction Proven review](https://mysubscriptionaddiction.com/proven-skincare-review)
- Funding: $6M seed round (Feb 2019) led by Belcorp Ventures, with Soma Capital, H&H Group, UpHonest Capital, Mindset Ventures; $12.2M Series A (reported Sept 2023) led by NewH2 Ventures — bringing total disclosed VC funding to roughly $21.3M — [Clay.com Proven funding dossier](https://www.clay.com/dossier/proven-skincare-funding); [GCI Magazine: Proven raises $12.2M Series A](https://www.gcimagazine.com/brands-products/news/news/22872769/proven-raises-122m-series-a-report)
- Separately, Proven launched a Regulation A+ offering seeking to raise up to $60M at $6.60/unit (2021) — a crowdfunding-style equity raise distinct from traditional VC rounds, and the $60M figure is the offering ceiling, not a confirmed amount actually raised — [BusinessWire: PROVEN $60M Reg A+ offering](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20211007005436/en/AI-Driven-PROVEN-Skincare-Announces-Launch-of-60-Million-Regulation-A-Offering)
- Traction/reviews: Proven Skincare has a 4.5/5 TrustScore from 4,477 Trustpilot reviews; the brand appeared on "Shark Tank" and walked away without a deal, while continuing to grow — [Trustpilot Proven Skincare](https://nz.trustpilot.com/review/provenskincare.com); [Beauty Independent: Shark Tank](https://www.beautyindependent.com/?p=40906)
- Continuous feedback: customer reviews explicitly note that "the personalized subscription approach, which adjusts products based on individual skin changes and feedback, sets the brand apart" — [RetailCoupons.com Proven Skincare reviews summary]
- Geography: Proven launched in Canada in July 2021, then announced expansion into the EU and UK in July 2022, making cleanser, moisturizer, night cream, and day/night eye creams purchasable directly by EU/UK customers — [BusinessWire: Proven international expansion EU/UK](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220719005333/en/PROVEN-Skincare-Announces-International-Expansion-Personalized-Adaptive-and-AI-Backed-Skincare-Products-Now-Available-in-the-European-Union-and-the-United-Kingdom)

### Inferences
- Proven markets itself as "customized"/"personalized," but available sources describe an algorithm selecting/weighting ingredient concentrations from a large data-driven decision space rather than confirming each order is an individually hand-mixed batch unique to one customer (contrast with Curology's compounding-pharmacy model or Atolla's per-customer serum mixing). This ambiguity matters directly for the "custom-formulated vs. off-the-shelf" key question and should be treated as unresolved rather than asserted either way.
- Because Proven is non-prescription/OTC, it can expand cross-border (Canada, UK, EU) far more easily than a prescription-compounding model like Curology's — explaining why Proven, not Curology, is the one of the two already selling into the EU.

### Gaps
- Exact founding year not confirmed from sourced material in this pass (funding history implies company existed by ~2018-2019 at the latest, given the Feb 2019 seed round).
- No source clearly confirms whether Proven's "customization" is a uniquely mixed batch per order or a selection among a finite set of pre-manufactured formula variants — this is a material gap for assessing how deep its "beyond diagnosis+recommend" claim really goes.
- No dedicated mobile app (iOS/Android) was confirmed in sources; Proven's quiz/ordering appears to be a web-based flow, so "app store rating" may not be applicable — flagged as unconfirmed rather than assumed.
- Whether Proven's EU/UK expansion specifically includes France (vs. UK/a subset of EU countries) was not explicitly confirmed.

## Company Profile: Trove Skin (TroveSkin) — Not Actually a US Company

### Takeaway
The only identifiable "TroveSkin" is a Singapore-headquartered AI photo-diagnosis app (branded "TroveSkin: Skincare Coach") that is overwhelmingly focused on Southeast Asia (Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand, Vietnam, Philippines) with 1.2 million registered users; no evidence was found of a distinct US-based company by this name, so — contrary to the task's framing — it does not appear to be a genuine US competitor, though its "photo diagnosis + recommend off-the-shelf products via e-commerce partners" architecture is a useful structural analog to consider.

### Cited Findings
- TroveSkin was launched in late 2017 and now has 1.2 million registered users, about 60% from Indonesia, with the remainder from Malaysia, Thailand, Vietnam and the Philippines; founder is Reuben Lee — [The Edge Singapore coverage via search synthesis](https://alfi.dev.theedgesingapore.com/news/startups-entrepreneurs-digital-economy/skincare-app-company-leverages-treasure-trove-data)
- TroveSkin is identified as Singapore-based (not Indonesia-based) in company-database coverage, i.e., Singapore HQ serving Southeast Asian markets — [CBInsights TroveSkin alternatives/competitors page](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/troveskin/alternatives-competitors)
- Product: the app uses AI to analyze a selfie and detect/track pores, wrinkles, pigmentation, sun damage and moisture levels; it also tracks product use and lifestyle habits to identify skin triggers, and recommends products available through e-commerce partners in local markets rather than manufacturing anything itself; it added a "TrovePoints" rewards program (survey participation redeemable for products) — [Banuba skincare-analysis-apps roundup](https://www.banuba.com/blog/best-skin-care-analysis-apps); search synthesis of The Edge Singapore coverage
- The app's current App Store listing title is "TroveSkin: Skincare Coach" (Apple ID 1435184918) — [AppShunter TroveSkin listing](https://appshunter.io/ios/app/troveskin-skincare-coach/id1435184918/similar)

### Inferences
- Because TroveSkin recommends existing third-party products (sold via local e-commerce partners) rather than manufacturing or compounding anything custom, its business model appears to monetize via affiliate/retail-referral commerce rather than direct subscription or product revenue — no subscription price point was found in any source, consistent with a free, ad/commission-supported app.
- Given its Southeast Asia concentration, TroveSkin is not a meaningful direct competitive threat to a France/EU-focused app like Glowy; it is more useful to the report writer as evidence of what a "diagnosis + recommend, no custom manufacturing" model looks like at meaningful user scale (1.2M registered users) outside the US/EU.

### Gaps
- No funding figures for TroveSkin were found despite it being tracked on CBInsights and Dealroom (listing pages exist but dollar amounts were not surfaced in available search snippets).
- It remains possible a separate, unrelated "Trove Skin" US company/app exists that this research did not surface under that exact name; multiple targeted searches (including "dermatologist," "United States," "app store availability") consistently returned only the Singapore/Southeast Asia TroveSkin, so confidence is moderate-to-high that no distinct major US entity by this name exists, but it is not absolutely ruled out.

## Company Profile: Function of Beauty ("Functionof") and Atolla

### Takeaway
"Functionof" almost certainly refers to **Function of Beauty**, a New York-based, MIT-founded (2015) mass-customization beauty company that mixes hair/skin/body products to order via its own automated manufacturing and has raised roughly $248M total; it **acquired Atolla in August 2021** specifically to add Atolla's patented at-home skin-testing technology and continuous-reformulation data engine to its manufacturing scale — meaning two of this assignment's five "competitors" are now one combined entity, with Atolla's original standalone subscription apparently discontinued post-acquisition.

### Cited Findings
- Function of Beauty: New York-based, founded 2015 out of MIT (Y Combinator W16), popularized mass-personalization in beauty; expanded from pure DTC into retailers like Target and CVS — [search synthesis]; [Y Combinator Function of Beauty profile](https://ycombinator.com/companies/function-of-beauty)
- Mechanism: customers take an online quiz (type, goals, preferences); an in-house algorithm/team mixes a made-to-order formula (including color/scent); the skincare line is marketed as offering "the greatest degree of customization in skin care on the market with over 3 billion possible formulations" — [search synthesis of Function of Beauty skincare marketing copy]
- Acquisition: Function of Beauty acquired Atolla on August 4, 2021, explicitly to combine "Atolla's patented skin care technology" (at-home test + data-driven recommendation engine) with Function of Beauty's "fully automated, industry-leading customized manufacturing capabilities" to create "the most advanced custom skin care offering available on the market" — [BeautyMatter: Function of Beauty acquires Atolla](https://beautymatter.com/articles/function-of-beauty-acquires-personalized-skincare-brand-atolla); [Beauty Packaging: same acquisition](https://www.beautypackaging.com/contents/view_breaking-news/2021-08-04/function-of-beauty-acquires-personalized-skin-care-company-atolla)
- Pricing (skincare line, as of Jan 2024): customized moisturizer starts at $45; skincare overall starts at $24.99 (for context, body wash/lotion from $21, hair care from $20) — [search synthesis of Function of Beauty pricing roundup, Jan 2024]
- Funding/scale: total funding raised is reported at approximately $247.9M; employee count estimates range 101-200 (one source) to 251-500 (another); 2023 full-year revenue was estimated in the $125-150M range by industry observers; the company hired a new CEO, Monica Belsito (former CMO at Sabra), in July 2025 specifically to drive DTC and wholesale growth — [CBInsights Function of Beauty financials](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/function-of-beauty/financials); search synthesis of 2025 CEO-hire coverage
- Manufacturing footprint: to support international growth, Function of Beauty expanded manufacturing to four facilities in Pennsylvania plus one in Michigan dedicated largely to international orders — [Glossy: inside Function of Beauty's international-expansion playbook](https://www.glossy.co/beauty/inside-function-of-beautys-playbook-for-international-expansion/)
- Geography: Function of Beauty ships to roughly 45 international markets, explicitly including Canada, Ireland, New Zealand, Australia, the UK, Sweden, Denmark, the Netherlands, Germany, **France**, Spain, and Italy; it also sells at UK retailer Cult Beauty (limited-customization base product priced ~£12 vs. ~£17 on its own DTC site) — [Glossy: Function of Beauty UK/Canada retail expansion](https://www.glossy.co/beauty/function-of-beauty-expands-retail-partnerships-in-u-k-and-canada/); [Cosmetics & Toiletries: Function of Beauty at Cult Beauty](https://www.cosmeticsandtoiletries.com/news/news/22444960/function-of-beauty-launches-at-cult-beauty)
- Atolla (pre-acquisition, standalone): founded 2017 by Meghan Maupin (MIT grad), data scientist Sid Salvi, and board-certified dermatologist Dr. Ranella Hirsch; product was the "Atolla Skin Health System" — an app-scannable paper test-strip kit measuring hydration, oil, pH and absorption, used monthly, paired with a custom serum reformulated based on each month's results — [search synthesis of Dezeen/BeautyMatter/CosmeticsDesign Atolla coverage](https://www.dezeen.com/2019/09/03/atolla-personalised-skin-care-products/)
- Atolla pricing (pre-acquisition): first month ~$65 (included the Initial Skin Health Kit), then $45/month for the ongoing custom serum — [search synthesis of Atolla subscription-review coverage]
- Atolla funding (pre-acquisition): $1.5M pre-seed (2019) plus a $2.5M seed round led by Lyra Growth Partners — roughly $4M total raised as an independent company — [Beauty Independent: Atolla secures $2.5M](https://www.beautyindependent.com/algorithm-powered-skincare-atolla-secures-funding/)
- A 2024-era third-party review source states that "Atolla has permanently closed down" as a reason current pricing is no longer available — consistent with the standalone Atolla brand/subscription being discontinued after its technology and team were absorbed into Function of Beauty — [search synthesis; exact closure date not independently confirmed via an official source]

### Inferences
- The task's framing treats "Functionof," "Atolla," and the other three as five independent competitors; in reality this is better modeled as **four** distinct competitive entities, since Atolla's technology/team is now inside Function of Beauty. The report writer should note this consolidation explicitly rather than scoring Atolla as a fully separate, currently-purchasable competitor.
- Function of Beauty/Atolla combined represents the deepest "beyond diagnosis+recommend" model in the non-prescription segment: true custom-mixed manufacturing (Function of Beauty) plus a patented continuous skin-retesting and reformulation loop (Atolla) — this pairing is architecturally the closest non-prescription analog to what a sophisticated AI-diagnosis-plus-custom-product app like Glowy might aspire to.
- Function of Beauty's confirmed direct shipping into France (among ~45 international markets) makes it the single most concretely-already-present-in-France competitor among the five originally assigned names — though it is not confirmed whether the *skincare* SKUs specifically (as opposed to the flagship haircare line) are included in that French availability.

### Gaps
- No official, dated announcement of Atolla's standalone shutdown was found (only an inferential statement in a third-party review); the exact timing and scope (did the Atolla app/brand name disappear entirely, or does Function of Beauty still sell an "Atolla-powered" test kit under its own brand?) is unconfirmed.
- Could not confirm whether Function of Beauty's *skincare* line (as opposed to its original and larger haircare business) is included in the ~45-market international shipping footprint, or whether skincare remains US-only even as haircare ships to France/EU.
- No independent confirmation was found of a brand literally named "Functionof" (without "Beauty") — this interpretation is treated as the most likely intended referent but should be flagged to the user/report writer as an assumption.

## Other Notable US (and Adjacent Non-US) Personalized-Skincare Competitors

### Takeaway
Beyond the five named companies, the most strategically significant adjacent player is **Apostrophe** — a Curology-style prescription telederm startup acquired by Hims & Hers for $190M in 2021 and then **shut down in March 2025** to consolidate into Hims & Hers' own teledermatology brand — a strong signal that this category is consolidating around a few large platforms. Two UK-based companies (Dermatica and Skin+Me) already run the Curology-style prescription-compounded model inside the UK/European regulatory environment, which is directly relevant context for assessing how exportable the US prescription playbook is to Europe.

### Cited Findings
- Apostrophe offered DTC personalized prescription skincare (tretinoin-based anti-aging, acne, melasma, rosacea treatments) prescribed by board-certified dermatology providers after patients answered async questions and uploaded photos; its verticalized pharmacy/compounding/fulfillment network was considered its key strength versus Hims — [Fierce Healthcare: Hims & Hers shutters Apostrophe](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/telehealth/hims-hers-shutters-apostrophe-favor-its-own-tele-dermatology-offerings); [Glossy: Hims acquires Apostrophe](https://www.glossy.co/beauty/hims-acquires-teledermatology-company-apostrophe/)
- Hims & Hers acquired Apostrophe for $190 million in 2021; Hims & Hers later shut the brand down "to simplify" its dermatology operations, with Apostrophe subscriptions canceled as of March 7, 2025 — [MobiHealthNews: Hims & Hers Apostrophe acquisition](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/hims-hers-dives-deeper-acne-care-apostrophe-acquisition); search synthesis of Fierce Healthcare shutdown coverage
- CureSkin is described as an AI-driven platform for personalized skincare/haircare that has raised $20.0 million in Series B funding — [search synthesis; geography (believed India-focused) was not confirmed by a citable source in this research pass]
- Openface is a personalized AI-based cosmetics startup that develops formulas using "precisely calibrated portions of various ingredients, selected on an individual basis," and raised $380K in a pre-seed round — [search synthesis of CBInsights Openface comparison pages]
- Dermatica: a UK personalized prescription-strength skincare brand founded 2018, owned by London healthtech company HeliosX; its team of dermatologists, pharmacists and GPs assesses patients and prescribes a monthly treatment shipped to their door; Dermatica expanded to the US in 2020 but is **permanently closing its US operations**, while continuing to operate in the UK "and parts of Europe"; Dermatica and Skin+Me are described as "the two best-known subscription services of this kind in the UK" — [Beauty Independent: Dermatica traveled from UK to US to take on Curology](https://www.beautyindependent.com/custom-prescription-skincare-service-dermatica-traveled-uk-to-us/); [Cosmetics Business: Dermatica to permanently close US operations](https://cosmeticsbusiness.com/dermatica-permanently-close-us-operations)
- Oh My Cream, a French beauty retailer, offers a "Beauty AI" tool (developed with French startup Dialog) that combines a personalized questionnaire with photo analysis to diagnose skin imbalances and recommend an evolving routine — i.e., a diagnosis-based personalization tool already live in the French retail market — [Industries Cosmétiques: Oh My Cream Beauty AI](https://www.industries-cosmetiques.fr/en/oh-my-cream-offers-a-beauty-ia-to-diagnose-skin-24-hours-a-day/)
- Haut.AI is a (non-US, European-linked) B2B AI skin-analysis technology provider — trained on 3 million images, assessing 150+ facial biomarkers — licensed to brands such as Beiersdorf and Ulta Beauty; it is an infrastructure/technology vendor rather than a consumer-facing subscription competitor — [search synthesis of Haut.AI coverage]

### Inferences
- The Apostrophe acquire-then-shutdown cycle is a meaningful cautionary data point: even a well-funded ($190M acquisition price), dermatologist-backed, prescription-compounding personalized-skincare brand was not durable as a standalone entity once its acquirer decided to consolidate around a single flagship brand — suggesting the "diagnosis + prescription + compounding" model's economics favor scale/platform consolidation over multiple competing standalone brands.
- The existence of Dermatica and Skin+Me shows the Curology-style regulated prescription model is **already operating inside European/UK regulatory frameworks**, which somewhat undercuts a "US prescription players can't easily threaten us in Europe" assumption — the playbook has already crossed the Atlantic in the other direction (UK to US, and UK stays in the UK/EU), and a similar US-to-EU move (e.g., Curology itself, or a fresh entrant copying Curology) is not structurally implausible even though none of the five assigned US companies has done so yet.
- Oh My Cream's Beauty AI is arguably a more directly relevant same-market signal for Glowy than any single one of the five US-named companies, since it demonstrates that French retail is already experimenting with photo+quiz-based skin diagnosis tools.

### Gaps
- Geography/HQ for CureSkin was not confirmed by a citable source in this pass (commonly associated with India in general industry knowledge, but this was not verified via search in this session and should not be asserted as fact without further confirmation).
- Openface's geography, product pricing, and current operating status were not confirmed.
- Whether Dermatica/Skin+Me's European coverage specifically includes France (vs. only the UK and a subset of EU countries) was not confirmed.
- Exact date of Dermatica's US closure announcement was not clearly dated to a specific year in sourced material (source pages referencing it carried 2026 posting metadata, suggesting the closure news is recent, but the precise announcement date was not isolated).

## Cross-Cutting: Custom-Formulated Products vs. Recommending Off-the-Shelf Products

### Takeaway
Three of the profiled companies genuinely manufacture/compound a product specific to the individual customer (Curology's compounded prescription cream, Function of Beauty's mixed-to-order cosmetic formulas, and Atolla's monthly-retested custom serum), while TroveSkin is a pure diagnosis-and-recommend model pointing users to existing third-party SKUs; Proven Skincare's marketing claims deep customization, but available sources do not clearly confirm whether each order is an individually mixed batch or a selection among a finite set of pre-made formula variants.

### Cited Findings
- See per-company Cited Findings above: Curology compounds a cream with up to three actives per prescription — [Curology profile](https://altss.com/profile/curology.md); Function of Beauty mixes formulas to order at its own Pennsylvania/Michigan facilities — [Glossy](https://www.glossy.co/beauty/inside-function-of-beautys-playbook-for-international-expansion/); Atolla formulated a custom serum based on monthly at-home test results — [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2019/09/03/atolla-personalised-skin-care-products/)
- TroveSkin explicitly recommends products "available on these [partner] sites" rather than manufacturing anything — [search synthesis of The Edge Singapore coverage]
- Proven's formulation process is described as algorithmically selecting ingredients/concentrations from a large data-driven decision space "formulated to order," but no source in this research pass explicitly confirmed per-customer unique batch mixing versus selection from a pre-set catalog of SKU variants — [search synthesis]

### Inferences
- A clean hierarchy emerges: (1) prescription-compounded (Curology, formerly Apostrophe) > (2) cosmetic mixed-to-order with proprietary in-house manufacturing (Function of Beauty, Atolla pre-acquisition) > (3) algorithmically-curated but ambiguously-"custom" (Proven) > (4) pure diagnosis+recommend of existing third-party products (TroveSkin). Glowy's own positioning should be benchmarked against which tier it occupies/aspires to.

### Gaps
- Definitive, primary-source confirmation of Proven's exact manufacturing granularity (true per-order bespoke batch vs. SKU-variant selection) was not found.

## Cross-Cutting: Licensed Dermatologist / Medical Provider Involvement and Regulatory Model

### Takeaway
Only the prescription-based players (Curology, and formerly Apostrombe/Apostrophe in the US; Dermatica/Skin+Me in the UK/Europe) involve a licensed medical provider and operate under drug/telehealth regulation; Proven, Function of Beauty, Atolla, and TroveSkin are all non-prescription, cosmetic-only, and do not involve a licensed medical provider in the loop.

### Cited Findings
- Curology: licensed medical providers (via telehealth) issue a compounded prescription; subject to FDA drug-labeling enforcement (2022 warning letter) — see Curology profile above.
- Apostrophe: prescriptions issued by "board-certified dermatology providers" via async telehealth consultation, with its own pharmacy network for compounding/fulfillment — [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/telehealth/hims-hers-shutters-apostrophe-favor-its-own-tele-dermatology-offerings)
- Dermatica (UK/Europe): a team of dermatologists, pharmacists and GPs assess patients and prescribe treatment under UK telehealth/medical regulation — [Beauty Independent](https://www.beautyindependent.com/custom-prescription-skincare-service-dermatica-traveled-uk-to-us/)
- Proven, Function of Beauty, and TroveSkin were not described in any source as involving a licensed medical provider; their products are marketed and sold as cosmetics, not drugs.
- Atolla's founding team included a board-certified dermatologist (Dr. Ranella Hirsch) as a co-founder/advisor, but the product itself (a cosmetic serum) was not sold as a prescription item and did not require an ongoing provider consultation per order — [search synthesis of Atolla founder coverage]

### Inferences
- The presence/absence of a licensed medical provider is the single clearest fault line in this competitive set, correlating directly with regulatory exposure (FDA drug rules in the US; MHRA/medical-prescribing rules in the UK) versus pure cosmetics regulation — this is a core strategic choice Glowy should weigh explicitly (deeper medical moat and higher regulatory burden vs. faster, lighter-weight cosmetic/algorithmic model).

### Gaps
- No explicit confirmation of which EU member-state regulatory frameworks (France's specifically) would govern a Curology/Dermatica-style compounded-prescription model if introduced in France — this would require dedicated EU pharma/telehealth regulatory research beyond this assignment's scope.

## Cross-Cutting: Scale and Funding Comparison (Threat-Level Indicator)

### Takeaway
By disclosed VC funding and scale, Function of Beauty (~$248M raised, ~$125-150M estimated 2023 revenue, 100-500 employees) is by far the largest and most resourced of the group, followed by Curology (~$30M raised, 5.5M+ patients served, ~423 employees), then Proven Skincare (~$21M VC raised plus an uncertain-outcome $60M Reg A+ offering), with Atolla (~$4M raised pre-acquisition, now folded into Function of Beauty) and TroveSkin (funding undisclosed, 1.2M users but concentrated in Southeast Asia) the smallest/least threatening on a pure-resources basis.

### Cited Findings
- Function of Beauty: ~$247.9M total raised; 2023 estimated revenue $125-150M; 101-500 employees (range across sources); founded 2015 — [CBInsights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/function-of-beauty/financials)
- Curology: ~$30M raised across two $15M rounds; 5.5M+ patients served; 423 employees; founded 2014 — see Curology section above.
- Proven Skincare: ~$21.3M VC raised ($6M seed + $12.2M Series A); separately sought up to $60M via Reg A+ offering (amount actually raised via that vehicle unconfirmed); founded year unconfirmed (active by 2019 at latest) — see Proven section above.

### Inferences

## Cross-Cutting: Continuous Feedback Loops (Reformulation Over Time)

### Takeaway
Atolla (pre-acquisition) had the most explicit, technically verifiable continuous feedback loop — a monthly at-home retest driving a reformulated serum each cycle; Proven Skincare's marketing and customer reviews describe an adaptive subscription that "adjusts products based on individual skin changes and feedback"; Curology's provider-based telehealth relationship plausibly supports ongoing formula adjustment but this specific mechanism was not explicitly documented in sources found this session; Function of Beauty's core flow is quiz-once-then-ship (continuous adjustment capability likely inherited from the Atolla acquisition but not independently confirmed as a shipped consumer feature); TroveSkin tracks skin changes over time but adjusts only its off-the-shelf product *recommendations*, not a manufactured formula.

### Cited Findings
- Atolla: "customers will test hydration, oil, pH, and absorption of their skin monthly and be sent a custom serum tailored to their current needs" — explicit monthly retest-and-reformulate loop — [search synthesis of Atolla product-mechanism coverage](https://www.dezeen.com/2019/09/03/atolla-personalised-skin-care-products/)
- Proven: customer-review synthesis states the "personalized subscription approach...adjusts products based on individual skin changes and feedback" — [RetailCoupons.com Proven Skincare reviews]
- TroveSkin: the app "allows users to track their skincare products, lifestyle choices, and habits to identify triggers and improve their skin health" over time, but its output remains a recommendation of existing products, not a reformulated custom product — [search synthesis]

### Inferences
- A genuine, hardware/test-driven continuous feedback loop (Atolla's model) is the most differentiated and defensible version of this feature in the dataset, because it is grounded in an objective repeated measurement (pH/oil/hydration strips) rather than subjective self-report alone — this is arguably the most relevant direct product-architecture analog for Glowy to study, even though Atolla itself appears to no longer operate as a standalone consumer product.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm from sources in this pass whether Curology's provider relationship includes a structured, named "check-in" or photo-reassessment process that triggers formula changes (this is commonly understood as part of comparable telederm models, but no explicit source was captured this session — treat as unconfirmed, not assumed).
- Could not confirm whether Function of Beauty's current skincare product actually ships with Atolla's monthly retest-and-reformulate mechanic as a live consumer feature, or whether that capability remains unlaunched/R&D-stage since the 2021 acquisition.

## Cross-Cutting: Geographic Presence — France/Europe Threat Assessment

### Takeaway
Of the originally-named five, Curology is explicitly US-only (no shipping even to Canada); Proven Skincare already ships directly to Canada, the UK and the EU (since July 2022); Function of Beauty ships to roughly 45 international markets including France, Germany, Spain and Italy (though it's unconfirmed whether this covers its skincare SKUs specifically); Atolla was US-only and is now discontinued/absorbed; and TroveSkin is a Southeast Asia-focused company with no US or Europe relevance at all. Separately, two non-US entities already active in Europe — Dermatica/Skin+Me (UK, Curology-style prescription model) and Oh My Cream (French retailer, photo+quiz diagnosis tool) — are arguably more immediately relevant same-region competitive signals for Glowy than most of the five US-named companies.

### Cited Findings
- Curology: US-only, cannot ship to Canada or anywhere else — see Curology section.
- Proven Skincare: launched in Canada (July 2021), expanded to EU and UK (July 2022), selling cleanser/moisturizer/night cream/eye creams directly to EU/UK customers — see Proven section.
- Function of Beauty: ships to ~45 international markets including Canada, Ireland, NZ, Australia, UK, Sweden, Denmark, Netherlands, Germany, **France**, Spain, Italy; sold at UK retailer Cult Beauty — see Function of Beauty section.
- Atolla: no evidence found of international shipping during its independent operation (implicitly US-only; now discontinued).
- TroveSkin: Singapore HQ, Southeast Asia-focused (Indonesia-majority user base); no US or Europe presence identified.
- Dermatica (UK): operates in the UK "and parts of Europe," running a Curology-style prescription-compounded telederm model under UK/European regulation, while closing only its US operations — see Other Notable Players section.
- Oh My Cream (France): French retailer already running a questionnaire-plus-photo-analysis skin-diagnosis tool ("Beauty AI," built with French startup Dialog) directly in the French market — see Other Notable Players section.

### Inferences
- Among the five originally-assigned companies, **none currently runs a prescription/medical personalized-skincare offer in France or the EU** — the deepest "beyond diagnosis+recommend" US playbook (Curology's compounded tretinoin model) has zero EU footprint today. The nearest real-world proof that this playbook works under European regulation comes from UK-based Dermatica/Skin+Me, not from any of the US-named targets.
- Function of Beauty and Proven Skincare are the two of the five with confirmed direct-to-France/EU shipping today, making them the most immediately "real" competitive presences for a France-based audience among this group — though both are non-prescription, algorithmic/cosmetic models rather than medical ones, so they compete with Glowy on the "AI diagnosis + personalized product" positioning rather than on medical credibility.
- Overall, this research supports treating the five assigned companies primarily as **US playbook references** (to study what "good" looks like in diagnosis depth, monetization, and custom manufacturing) rather than as **direct in-market competitors** in France today — with Function of Beauty and Proven Skincare as partial exceptions due to their confirmed EU shipping.

### Gaps
- It was not confirmed whether Function of Beauty's shipping to France specifically includes its skincare product line (vs. only haircare).
- It was not confirmed whether Dermatica/Skin+Me's "parts of Europe" coverage includes France specifically.
- No evidence was found (or specifically searched in-depth) on whether Curology, Proven, or Function of Beauty have any stated plans or rumors regarding future France-specific market entry or localization (e.g., French-language quiz, EU cosmetics-regulation (CPNP) compliance) — this would be a useful follow-up research question if deeper France-specific risk assessment is needed.
