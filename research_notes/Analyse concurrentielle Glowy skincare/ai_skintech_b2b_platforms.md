## What is each vendor's business model — B2B licensing vs. direct-to-consumer app — and how do they monetize?

### Takeaway
Perfect Corp is explicitly a hybrid B2C + B2B company (its own consumer apps plus enterprise SaaS/API); Haut.AI and Revieve are fundamentally B2B SaaS/licensing businesses serving brands and retailers (Haut.AI has only recently added a small consumer-facing educational offshoot); Modiface is now an internal L'Oréal capability monetized indirectly through L'Oréal's own brand sales, with some external AR licensing; Newtone/QIMA is a B2B clinical-research service, not a retail or consumer product at all.

### Cited Findings
- Perfect Corp (NYSE: PERF) is described in its own investor materials as "a global leader in providing artificial intelligence (AI) and augmented reality (AR) Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) solutions to the beauty and fashion industries," operating consumer-facing YouCam mobile apps (YouCam Makeup, YouCam Perfect, etc.) alongside an enterprise B2B SaaS platform (virtual try-on, skin diagnostics, hair/accessory simulation, 3D authoring tools) — [Perfect Corp Q4 2024 results via search aggregation](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250226897274/en/Perfect-Corp.-Reports-Unaudited-Financial-Results-for-the-Three-Months-and-Full-Year-Ended-December-31-2024)
- On the B2B side, Perfect Corp's YouCam AI API customers "pay on a per-usage basis for certain API-based services" (unit/credit-based pricing); the consumer apps are the larger and faster-growing revenue driver, with Q3 2025 consumer app revenue hitting $18.7M (+15.7% YoY) vs. a "stabilizing" (slower-growing) B2B segment — [Zacks SCR Research via search aggregation](https://scr.zacks.com/news/news-details/2025/PERF-App-Revenues-Continue-to-Beat-Expectations-While-the-B2B-Business-Stabilizes/default.aspx)
- Perfect Corp's YouCam AI API is marketed as a "developer-first" platform with free account registration and purchasable/subscribable usage units, positioned (per a sponsored tech article) as accessible to e-commerce startups, not only large enterprises — [TechBullion](https://techbullion.com/why-youcam-ai-api-is-the-secret-weapon-for-e-commerce-startups/)
- Haut.AI describes itself as a "B2B SaaS" skincare-personalization platform; its 2023 €2M funding round was explicitly earmarked "to expand in skincare B2B SaaS," including a "no-code skincare e-commerce personalization software" for retailers, brands and clinics — [BusinessWire/CosmeticsDesign-Europe](https://www.cosmeticsdesign-europe.com/Article/2023/11/15/We-re-democratising-access-to-precise-skincare-analysis-Generative-AI-startup-Haut.AI-raises-2m-to-expand)
- Haut.AI's SkinGPT generative-AI tool was launched commercially as "a B2B innovation for brands"; in parallel the company introduced a limited **consumer-facing** companion site, "Generative.Skin: Library of Ingredient Effects," which lets consumers explore skincare-ingredient effects (educational/marketing tool, not a full personalized-diagnosis-and-shopping app) — [search aggregation of Haut.AI press coverage]
- Haut.AI also sells a separate "Clinical Studies Software" product aimed at accelerating large-scale skin research (claims substantiation) across clinic, hybrid and at-home settings — a B2B-to-CRO/brand-R&D offering distinct from its e-commerce personalization SaaS — [American Spa](https://www.americanspa.com/technology/new-ai-powered-clinical-software-can-fast-forward-skin-research)
- Revieve's business, based on all case studies found (J.C. Penney, Boots/No7, Murad), is structured as white-label digital-advisor software embedded into retailer/brand e-commerce sites and apps — there is no evidence in the sources reviewed of Revieve operating its own branded consumer-facing app.
- Modiface's revenue is not reported separately by L'Oréal (it is a wholly owned subsidiary); its economics are monetized indirectly via improved conversion/engagement on L'Oréal-owned brand sites and apps, with occasional external licensing deals (e.g., Amazon AR try-on, Coty brands) representing a secondary line of business — [Glossy](https://www.glossy.co/beauty/modiface-is-becoming-the-go-to-provider-of-augmented-reality-to-beauty-brands)
- Newtone Technologies (founded 2005) joined QIMA Life Sciences in 2021; its flagship ColorFace® product is a dedicated facial-imaging system sold to cosmetics companies and contract research organizations (CROs) to scientifically quantify product efficacy (acne, dark spots/circles, pores, redness, roughness, whitening, wrinkles) for regulatory/marketing claims substantiation in clinical studies — this is a B2B scientific-instrumentation/CRO-services model, not a retail-personalization or consumer app model — [QIMA Life Sciences](https://qima-lifesciences.com/?p=312426); [SGS brochure](https://campaigns.sgs.com/-/media/global/documents/brochures/sgs-crs-skincare-efficacy-newtone-colorface-en.pdf)

### Inferences
- Perfect Corp is the only one of the four named companies with a large, monetizing, direct-to-consumer app business of its own — meaning it is simultaneously a potential Glowy technology supplier (via its API) and a direct consumer-app competitor (via YouCam Makeup/Beauty Advisor's own skin-diagnosis features).
- Revieve and Newtone/QIMA pose essentially no direct-to-consumer competitive threat (no branded consumer app), making them purer "potential technology partner/vendor" plays for Glowy; Haut.AI sits in between, with a small consumer-facing educational offshoot but no full competing diagnosis-and-commerce app identified.

### Gaps
- Exact B2B pricing/contract structures (minimum contract values, per-scan costs, tiering) are not publicly disclosed by any of the four vendors in the sources found; this limits precise cost estimation for a potential Glowy licensing deal (see dedicated section below).
- Could not confirm whether Revieve has ever operated a direct-to-consumer branded app (its own site, revieve.com, was blocked from direct fetch in this environment).

## What skin metrics/conditions can each platform's AI detect from a photo, and how validated/accurate is this claimed to be?

### Takeaway
Perfect Corp has the most extensive publicly documented metric list (15+ conditions) and the most concrete, named-academic validation study (Wake Forest dermatologist); Haut.AI has the most methodologically transparent repeatability study (via an independent clinical-research institute) but a marketing accuracy claim that is not independently backed; L'Oréal/Modiface's Vichy tool is dermatologist-developed with an ethnically diverse training set but has no publicly available accuracy percentage; Newtone/QIMA is positioned as "clinical-grade" by nature of its instrumentation-and-CRO business but no consumer-facing accuracy figure was found.

### Cited Findings
- Perfect Corp's current YouCam AI Skin Analysis API is advertised as detecting 15+ skin concerns: spots, wrinkles, texture, dark circles, redness, pores, acne, oiliness, hydration, firmness, droopy eyelids, eye bags, radiance and tear trough; the API is stated to be Vanta-certified HIPAA-compliant and GDPR-compliant — [Perfect Corp YouCam Enterprise site via search aggregation](https://yce.perfectcorp.com/features/skin-analysis-api)
- Perfect Corp's 2020-era YouCam Skin Score diagnosed 8 conditions (moisture, oiliness, redness, acne, spots, wrinkles, texture, dark circles) and was "developed using over 70,000 clinical images" plus a patented "AgileFace®" facial-tracking technology — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20200925005178/en/Perfect-Corp.-Releases-the-Next-Generation-of-YouCam%E2%80%99s-AI-Skin-Diagnostic-Solution-to-Help-Beauty-Brands-Recommend-Personalized-Skincare-Regimens-for-Their-Consumers)
- A validation study attributed to Dr. Steven Feldman, professor of dermatology at Wake Forest School of Medicine, reported: 95% test-retest reliability for Perfect Corp's AI Skin technology; agreement with physician assessments highest for redness, wrinkles and eye bags (83.7% each) and radiance (81.6%); and higher clinician agreement for the AI tool (69%) than for a "dedicated clinical hardware imaging system" (56%) in the same comparison — roughly 19% higher accuracy than that specific hardware benchmark — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220914005558/en/New-Scientific-Research-Confirms-High-Accuracy-of-Perfect-Corp.-AI-Skin-Technology-for-Evaluating-Skin-Concerns-in-Dermatology-Settings); [PersonalCareInsights](https://www.personalcareinsights.com/news/perfect-corp-study-finds-ai-powered-skincare-imaging-at-par-with-conventional-analysis-methods.html)
- Haut.AI's skin-measurement technology was evaluated against a consensus panel of expert graders at the Institut d'Expertise Clinique (I.E.C.), reportedly achieving ICC (intraclass correlation coefficient) values of 0.97–0.98 — described as "excellent" repeatability — across five facial skin endpoints, with agreement holding whether images were captured on medical-grade equipment or on a participant's own phone at home — [Haut.AI clinical-software materials via search aggregation](https://haut.ai/products/clinical-software)
- Haut.AI's technology is described as trained on a dataset of "over 3 million diverse images," developed in collaboration with dermatologists — [search aggregation]
- Separately, Haut.AI's own marketing is reported to claim "98% diagnostic accuracy," but an independent trade article found no peer-reviewed study in academic databases supporting that specific number — a notable gap between marketing claims and the (narrower, repeatability-focused) published validation data — [theacare.de](https://www.theacare.de/post/how-accurate-is-ai-skin-analysis-what-skincare-brands-should-know)
- L'Oréal/Modiface's AI skin-diagnostic algorithm (first deployed as Vichy SkinConsultAI) detects seven signs of aging: under-eye wrinkles, lack of firmness, fine lines, lack of radiance, dark spots, deep wrinkles, and pores. It was trained via deep learning on 6,000 clinical images from L'Oréal R&I's "Skin Aging Atlases" studies, then refined using more than 4,500 smartphone selfies spanning three population groups (Asian, Caucasian, Afro-American) under four different lighting conditions, and was developed in partnership with dermatologists — [L'Oréal newsroom via search aggregation](https://www.loreal.com/en/news/research-innovation/loreal-and-modiface-an-artificial-intelligencepowered-skin-diagnostic)
- Newtone's ColorFace® system is marketed for precise quantification of skincare-product-driven improvements across acne, dark spots/circles, pores, redness, roughness, whitening and wrinkles, positioned for scientific/regulatory claims substantiation rather than for a casual consumer photo-scan use case — [QIMA Life Sciences](https://qima-lifesciences.com/?p=312426)

### Inferences
- Perfect Corp currently has the broadest publicly listed metric set (15+ concerns) of the vendors reviewed, slightly ahead of Modiface's 7 aging-specific signs and the generic descriptions given for Haut.AI and Newtone.
- Across the industry, "accuracy" claims are inconsistently defined (test-retest reliability vs. physician-agreement percentage vs. ICC repeatability vs. unbacked marketing percentages), making like-for-like comparison difficult; Perfect Corp's study is the most concrete in naming a specific comparator (a dedicated clinical hardware system) and a named academic validator.

### Gaps
- No specific accuracy percentage or sample size for L'Oréal/Modiface's Vichy tool beyond training-set size was found in the sources reviewed (loreal.com could not be directly fetched in this environment).
- No consumer-facing accuracy benchmark was found for Newtone/QIMA's ColorFace, consistent with it being a clinical-research instrument rather than a consumer product.
- Could not verify whether any of these validation studies have been published in a peer-reviewed journal (as opposed to company-commissioned/company-distributed press releases) — this should be treated as a meaningful caveat when citing any of these accuracy figures in the final report.

## What is each company's traction, funding history, and founding background?

### Takeaway
Perfect Corp is the largest and most mature player by far (founded 2015, SPAC-listed on NYSE in 2022, ~$60M 2024 revenue, 732 cumulative brand clients) but is now going private in a 2026 buyout at a relatively modest valuation; Revieve and Haut.AI are much smaller venture-backed startups with single-digit-million-euro/dollar total disclosed funding; Modiface's financials are folded into L'Oréal and not disclosed separately; Newtone is a 20-year-old clinical-imaging specialist now absorbed into QIMA Life Sciences.

### Cited Findings
- Perfect Corp was founded in 2015 and went public in October 2022 via a SPAC merger with Provident Acquisition Corp., valued at approximately $1.02 billion, seeking $335 million in gross proceeds — [MarketingDive/Retail Dive via search aggregation](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/perfect-spac-financing-drive-ar-virtual-growth-beauty/620077/)

Chang; the price represents a ~48.1% premium to the pre-announcement closing price and ~39.6% to the 30-day VWAP; Chang's entities plus CyberLink International Technology Corp. together held ~53.4% of share capital and ~81.2% of voting power; the deal is expected to close Q4 2026, after which Perfect Corp delists from the NYSE — [Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/perfect-corp-agrees-to-2-per-share-going-private-merger-led-by-founder-alice-chang/); [InsideArbitrage](https://www.insidearbitrage.com/2026/07/ceo-alice-chang-led-projectny-to-take-perfect-private-for-65-67-million-in-cash/?c=ia&s1=tw)

Penney, Boots) — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/revieve/financials)
- Haut.AI raised €2 million in a 2023 seed-stage round from LongeVC and the venture-capital arm of Grupo Boticário's shareholders, earmarked for international expansion of its B2B SaaS skincare-personalization and generative-AI (SkinGPT) products — [BusinessWire/CosmeticsAndToiletries](https://www.cosmeticsandtoiletries.com/news/companies/news/22879308/hautai-gains-funding-from-longevc-and-grupo-boticario-vc-arm-for-generative-ai-skin-hair-platform)

- Newtone Technologies was founded in 2005 and specializes in clinical imaging for skin/hair-appearance evaluation; it joined QIMA Life Sciences in 2021 to strengthen QIMA's product-performance-evaluation services for clinical studies — [QIMA Life Sciences](https://qima-lifesciences.com/?p=312426); [PitchBook profile](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/181323-01)
- L'Oréal's Modiface acquisition (March 2018) was L'Oréal's first-ever technology-company acquisition; no separate funding/revenue figures for Modiface are disclosed post-acquisition, as it operates as a wholly owned subsidiary — [Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/news/loreal-buys-ar-beauty-tech-startup-modiface/519426/)

### Gaps

## Is there a clear technology leader in AI skin-diagnosis accuracy, and what is it based on?

### Takeaway
No independently peer-reviewed, head-to-head benchmark comparing Perfect Corp, Haut.AI, Revieve, Modiface/L'Oréal and Newtone/QIMA was found; each vendor publishes its own company-commissioned validation study using different metrics (physician-agreement %, ICC repeatability, training-set description), making direct comparison unreliable — Perfect Corp has the most detailed, named-academic study, while Haut.AI has a documented gap between its marketing accuracy claim and its published (narrower) validation data.

### Cited Findings
- See the detailed figures already cited above: Perfect Corp (95% test-retest reliability; 83.7% physician-agreement on several conditions; Wake Forest dermatologist Dr. Steven Feldman as named validator) — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220914005558/en/New-Scientific-Research-Confirms-High-Accuracy-of-Perfect-Corp.-AI-Skin-Technology-for-Evaluating-Skin-Concerns-in-Dermatology-Settings)
- Haut.AI (ICC 0.97–0.98 repeatability via Institut d'Expertise Clinique panel, but an unverified "98% diagnostic accuracy" marketing claim) — [theacare.de](https://www.theacare.de/post/how-accurate-is-ai-skin-analysis-what-skincare-brands-should-know)
- L'Oréal/Modiface describes its tool as achieving "a high level of skin assessment precision" with "accurate results" across varied real-world photo conditions, developed with dermatologists, but no specific percentage accuracy metric was located — [L'Oréal newsroom via search aggregation]
- No accuracy benchmark specific to a consumer-facing use case was found for Newtone/QIMA's ColorFace (it is positioned for scientific claims substantiation, which implies rigor but is not the same validation context as a casual consumer-app photo scan).

### Inferences
- Perfect Corp's validation data is the most citable and specific of the four (named academic, named institution, concrete percentages, explicit comparator), which — combined with its market-leading client count (732) and 10-year track record — makes it the most defensible candidate for "technology leader" by publicly available evidence, though this is based on company-commissioned/company-distributed research rather than independent, peer-reviewed replication.
- The overall pattern across the category (strong marketing claims, thinner independently-verifiable backing) suggests Glowy should not assume any vendor's published accuracy numbers are directly comparable to clinical-grade dermatology diagnostics, and should treat "AI skin diagnosis accuracy" claims industry-wide with some skepticism when benchmarking its own product.

### Gaps
- No independent, third-party (non-vendor-commissioned) comparative study of these AI skin-diagnosis tools' accuracy was found.
- Peer-review status of the Perfect Corp Wake Forest study and the Haut.AI/IEC study could not be confirmed (i.e., whether published in a journal vs. distributed only as a press release/white paper).

## Could Glowy realistically license one of these technologies rather than building its own — what would that cost/business model look like?

### Takeaway
Of the vendors reviewed, Perfect Corp's YouCam AI API is the most clearly accessible for a smaller app via self-serve registration and usage-based (per-call/credit) billing, and Haut.AI's SaaS is also explicitly positioned for brands/retailers (not only giants); Revieve and Newtone/QIMA appear structured around larger, custom enterprise-sales relationships (and, for Newtone, a different use case entirely) that would likely be a poorer fit or harder/costlier entry point for an early-stage consumer app like Glowy.

### Cited Findings
- Perfect Corp's YouCam AI API is described as a "developer-first AI platform" with a "scalable, RESTful architecture" letting businesses integrate skin-diagnosis and related beauty-AI features into their own apps/websites; accounts can be registered for free, after which usage is purchased/subscribed in units — [search aggregation of yce.perfectcorp.com and docs.perfectcorp.com]
- A sponsored trade article frames the YouCam AI API explicitly as viable for "e-commerce startups," not only large enterprise brands — [TechBullion](https://techbullion.com/why-youcam-ai-api-is-the-secret-weapon-for-e-commerce-startups/)
- Perfect Corp's API is marketed as Vanta-certified HIPAA-compliant and GDPR-compliant, which could reduce Glowy's own compliance burden if licensed — [search aggregation]
- Haut.AI explicitly markets its platform as "no-code" skincare e-commerce personalization software for "retailers, brands and clinics," suggesting a lower technical-integration bar than a fully custom-built in-house model — [CosmeticsDesign-Europe](https://www.cosmeticsdesign-europe.com/Article/2023/11/15/We-re-democratising-access-to-precise-skincare-analysis-Generative-AI-startup-Haut.AI-raises-2m-to-expand)
- Revieve's publicly documented case studies are all with large, established retail chains (J.C. Penney, Boots) — consistent with an enterprise, custom-contract go-to-market motion rather than a self-serve API product; no self-serve pricing or small-business-tier product was found in the sources reviewed.
- Newtone/QIMA's ColorFace is sold into cosmetics-company R&D and CRO workflows for regulatory claims studies — a fundamentally different commercial motion (clinical-study contracts) from a consumer-app real-time photo-scan feature, making it a poor licensing fit for Glowy's core product.

### Inferences
- If Glowy wanted to license rather than build, Perfect Corp's YouCam AI API is the most plausible near-term option based on publicly available go-to-market signals (self-serve account creation, usage-based pricing, explicit SMB/startup-friendly messaging), though actual negotiated pricing at Glowy's likely usage volume is not publicly disclosed and would need direct vendor contact to quote.

- Licensing either vendor would trade Glowy's current technology-differentiation story (presumably proprietary AI) for a reliance on a third party whose technology (and per-scan or per-API-call pricing) could also be licensed by any well-funded competitor or retailer — i.e., licensing lowers Glowy's build cost/time but does not create a durable moat, since the same API is available to others.

### Gaps
- No public pricing figures (cost per scan, monthly/annual minimums, revenue-share terms) were found for any of the four vendors' B2B/API products; this is the single biggest piece of missing information for a cost/business-model estimate and would require direct outreach to each vendor's sales team to quote.
